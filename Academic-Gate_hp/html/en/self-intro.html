02 — Research

Research

My field is early-universe cosmology. I study theoretically how the axion — a leading candidate for dark matter — was produced at the beginning of the universe, and how much of it remains today. If the axion energy density in the early universe greatly exceeds the maximum potential energy, the axion behaves like radiation even when its momentum is much smaller than its mass. As its energy density drops to the scale of the potential, a nonlinear transition occurs, after which the axion behaves as matter and can make up the dark matter. Our paper revisits the abundance and spectrum of axion dark matter by incorporating the effects of this nonlinear transition, and also discusses related phenomena such as the formation of primordial black holes. It was published in the Journal of High Energy Physics.
